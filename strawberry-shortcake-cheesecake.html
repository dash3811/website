Strawberry Shortcake Cheesecake Ice Cream

Yield: About 1 quart

Ingredients

Roasted Strawberries

  • 1 lb fresh strawberries, hulled and quartered
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt

Strawberry Cheesecake Ice Cream Base

  • 2 cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 3 tbsp nonfat dry milk powder
  • 1/3 cup granulated sugar
  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 4 oz cream cheese, softened
  • 1 tsp vanilla bean paste or extract
  • 1/4 tsp kosher salt
  • 2/3 of roasted strawberries, pureed
  • 2 tbsp freeze-dried strawberry powder (optional, highly recommended)

Strawberry Ribbon

  • 1/3 of roasted strawberries
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
  • Pinch of salt

Buttery Shortcake Crumble

  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 3 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 4 tbsp cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp heavy cream
  • 1 tbsp melted butter (optional)
  • Pinch flaky sea salt (optional)

Directions

1. Roast the Strawberries

  1. Preheat oven to 375°F.
  2. Hull and quarter the strawberries. Toss with sugar, lemon juice, vanilla, and salt.
  3. Spread in a single layer on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
  4. Roast 25–35 minutes, stirring once halfway through, until very soft and the juices are thick and syrupy.
  5. Cool. Reserve about 1/3 for the strawberry ribbon and puree the remaining 2/3 until smooth.

2. Make the Strawberry Cheesecake Base

  1. Combine heavy cream, whole milk, and nonfat dry milk powder in a saucepan. Whisk thoroughly to dissolve the milk powder.
  2. Heat over medium heat until steaming but not boiling.
  3. In a separate bowl, whisk egg yolks with 1/3 cup sugar.
  4. Slowly whisk about 1 cup hot dairy into the egg yolks to temper them.
  5. Pour everything back into the saucepan and cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until the custard reaches 170–175°F.
  6. Remove from heat. Add cream cheese, vanilla, kosher salt, strawberry puree, and optional freeze-dried strawberry powder.
  7. Blend with an immersion blender until completely smooth.
  8. Cover and refrigerate for at least 8 hours or overnight.

3. Make the Strawberry Ribbon

  1. Combine the reserved roasted strawberries, 1 tablespoon sugar, lemon juice, and a pinch of salt in a small saucepan.
  2. Cook over medium-low heat for 5–8 minutes, crushing the strawberries as they cook.
  3. Continue cooking until the mixture resembles thick strawberry preserves.
  4. Puree for a smooth ribbon or leave a few tiny strawberry pieces for texture.
  5. Cool completely before using.

4. Make the Shortcake Crumble

  1. Preheat oven to 350°F.
  2. Combine flour, sugar, and salt.
  3. Cut in the cold butter until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs.
  4. Add vanilla and heavy cream and gently mix until irregular clusters form.
  5. Spread on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
  6. Bake 12–16 minutes, stirring once halfway through, until golden brown and crisp.
  7. Cool completely. For extra buttery flavor, optionally toss with 1 tablespoon melted butter and a small pinch of flaky sea salt.

5. Churn the Ice Cream

  1. Pour the thoroughly chilled strawberry cheesecake base into your ice cream maker and churn according to the manufacturer's directions.
  2. During the final minute, add about 1/2 cup shortcake crumble.

6. Add the Strawberry Ribbon

  1. Transfer about 1 inch of churned ice cream into a freezer-safe container.
  2. Spoon thin ribbons of strawberry sauce over the ice cream.
  3. Continue alternating layers of ice cream and strawberry ribbon, using about 1/4 cup ribbon total.
  4. For more pronounced swirls, make one or two gentle passes with a butter knife. Do not stir.
  5. Freeze for 4–6 hours or until firm.

Tips & Upgrades

Freeze-Dried Strawberries

Highly recommended: Crush freeze-dried strawberries into a fine powder and add 2 tablespoons to the base when you add the roasted strawberry puree. This gives you concentrated strawberry flavor without adding extra water or much additional sugar. It is especially useful when making milkshakes because the strawberry flavor stays strong after milk is added.

Why Roast the Strawberries?

Roasting concentrates the strawberry flavor while cooking off excess water. That gives the ice cream a stronger fruit flavor and helps reduce iciness.

Why Use Milk Powder?

The 3 tablespoons of nonfat dry milk powder increase the milk solids and body without adding extra water. The result is creamier ice cream and a thicker, smoother milkshake.

Get a Better Strawberry Ribbon

Cook the ribbon until it is thick like preserves and let it cool completely before layering. If it is warm or too thin, it will blend into the ice cream instead of staying in distinct ribbons.

Better Shortcake Crumble

Let the crumble cool completely before adding it. For a stronger buttery shortcake flavor, toss the cooled crumble with 1 tablespoon melted butter and a tiny pinch of flaky sea salt.

Keep the Swirl Distinct

Layer the strawberry ribbon rather than stirring it into the ice cream. One or two gentle passes with a butter knife are plenty.

For a Classic Diner-Style Milkshake

Blend 3 generous scoops of this ice cream with 1/4–1/3 cup whole milk, 1 tablespoon shortcake crumble, and 1 teaspoon strawberry ribbon. Skip malted milk powder on the first shake so the strawberry-cheesecake flavor stays front and center.
